Gloria the chicken had always considered herself the most sophisticated bird on Maplewood Farm, so when the farmer once again strolled into the coop, pocketed her freshly laid egg, and left without a single word of appreciation, she decided enough was enough. With a dramatic swish of her tail feathers, she marched across the yard, past the cows who were gossiping about a broken fence, past the goats who were arguing about who chewed what, and barged right into the farmers kitchen. She flapped onto a chair, slammed her wings on the table, and announced that she was filing an official complaint. The farmer nearly dropped his mug. Chickens did not talk like this. Gloria began lecturing him on worker dignity, fair compensation, and emotional support for creative producers like herself. The farmer rubbed his eyes, convinced the ducks had been teaching her new words again since they were always watching television through the living room window.


Before he could respond, a pig wearing a tiny tie and carrying a folder walked in. This was Mr Oinkstein, Glorias self appointed attorney, who claimed to specialize in farm negotiations. He cleared his throat and read Glorias demands. She wanted formal compliments for every egg laid, at least three descriptive words per compliment, weekly corn bonuses, preferably seasoned, and installation of a fully heated mud spa for the improvement of poultry morale. The farmer pointed out they already had mud, but Mr Oinkstein calmly replied that unheated mud was simply dirt soup. A long negotiation followed, interrupted once when a goat attempted to eat the legal papers. In the end they reached an agreement. Gloria would stop crowing at four in the morning and wait for sunrise, and in return the farmer would provide the corn bonuses, the elaborate daily praise, and arrange for a very confused electrician to install a mud heater.


From that day on, every morning began with Gloria presenting her new egg as if it were a priceless treasure, and the farmer dutifully responding with glowing compliments such as magnificent and radiant. Gloria pretended to be modest but always strutted away with a proud little wiggle. Meanwhile, Mr Oinkstein enjoyed his new reputation as the best lawyer on the farm, even though he was paid only in apples. The ducks continued to watch television through the window, reviewing the events as if it were their favorite show. They gave the farmers performance a nine out of ten, deducting one point because he still had not installed better channels for them.
